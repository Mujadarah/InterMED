# Mediately (Romania) product analysis vs InterMED

Research date: **2026-10-20**. Status: **research proposal; no feature, data source, vendor contact or clinical decision is approved by this document.** Requested by the owner (a surgeon, InterMED's clinical reviewer) to find anything useful for InterMED in the Mediately Romanian service.

Method and evidence labels follow the existing research documents: reviewed facts cite the exact public URL where they were seen; **inference** marks a conclusion not stated by the source; **unverified** means the reviewed public material did not establish the fact (collapsed help content, login-gated or app-only behavior), not that the feature cannot exist. Public pages were fetched once each; no sign-up, login, form submission or high-volume crawling was performed. No Mediately content (drug texts, monographs, calculator or interaction text) is reproduced here; features are summarized in our own words, with at most one short attributed quote.

## 2. Data sources and licensing signals

- **Drug catalogue sources (cited, not proven):** the Romanian homepage cites ANMDM, CNAS and EMA as sources for the centralized medicine register ([homepage](https://mediately.co/ro/home)). This is a citation of provenance, not a rights statement — it does not show which fields come from which list, or update dates per field. **Inference:** the Romanian catalogue is regulator-fed (ANMDMR) with reimbursement/compensation enrichment (CNAS) and EU product-information references (EMA); the split per field is unverified.
- **Interaction data provider:** the Romanian interactions page attributes content to Medbase (link to `medbase.fi` in the page footer) ([interactions](https://mediately.co/ro/interactions)). Medbase's underlying dataset, severity vocabulary, update cadence and licence terms were **not** reviewed here (outside the permitted Mediately pages); no conclusion about its clinical quality is drawn. One page-source observation: the public stylesheet defines interaction-severity color classes labeled a, b, c and d (plus green and blue classes), consistent with a letter-graded severity display; the legend itself is client-rendered and **unverified**.
- **Reimbursement/compensation and prescribing-restriction data:** the data-licensing page sells "structured drug data" described as SPCs, reimbursement information, prescribing restrictions, EMA approvals and national drug records for 13 countries including Romania, delivered through one API ([data licensing](https://mediately.co/ro/data-licensing)). Observed fact: Mediately advertises a structured, licensed dataset that includes Romanian reimbursement and prescribing-restriction information. Which public sources feed it, whether those sources are openly licensed, and whether they are machine-readable are all **unverified**; the reviewed pages do not name them.
- **Provenance and versions:** tool pages carry a visible version (the BMI tool showed version 4) and a references section with citations and access dates ([BMI tool](https://mediately.co/ro/tools/BMI)). No dataset version/update date display for the drug catalogue was observed; whether drug pages show provenance and update dates is **unverified** (section 8).
- **Disclaimers:** the EULA disclaims warranty and states the software is provided "as is"; the product is positioned for educational and informational purposes and not medical advice (EULA §18.1); each tool repeats an educational-use warning (observed on the BMI tool). The AI assistant carries an extensive disclaimer set (third-party technology, no human review, no professional advice).
- **Reuse signals:** the EULA reserves all intellectual-property rights, grants no source-code access, and prohibits copying, renting, sublicensing, translating, combining, adapting or modifying, making the software available to others, and resale use (EULA §§1.2, 9, 10, 16.2). Governing law is Slovenia with exclusive Slovenian jurisdiction (EULA §20.6). These terms confirm the existing repository policy: public availability is not permission to reuse (see [DATA_SOURCES](../../plan/DATA_SOURCES.md) and requirement 22 in [REQUIREMENTS](../../plan/REQUIREMENTS.md)).

## 3. UX patterns worth learning from

Described in our own words; none of this is copied content.

1. **One entry point, many answer types.** The Romanian home frames a single search box covering drugs, CIM codes and tools, with the AI mode as an alternate entry. For InterMED this maps onto the existing local universal search (milestone 7 Part A) extended with calculator entries — a navigation decision, not a data feature.
2. **Interaction checker input model.** A substance-centric list (2 to 40 actives) with image search, provider attribution in the footer, and explicit help about what "no interaction found" means. The bounded input (2–40) and explicit semantics match InterMED's milestone-10 requirements (bounded input/no silent truncation; "no record" is not "safe"); seeing it in a competitor confirms these are usability essentials, not over-engineering.
3. **Calculator transparency.** The tool page surfaces, in one place: an educational warning banner, the classification thresholds, the formulas, cited references with access dates, and a visible tool version. InterMED already requires source/version/units/limitations on results (requirement 96); Mediately demonstrates the minimal presentation pattern a clinician expects. Its practice of printing references with access dates is a cheap, high-trust addition.
4. **Versioned tools.** Each calculator carries its own version number (BMI tool version 4), so a changed formula is visible. InterMED already versions formula components per approval record; the lesson is to expose that version in the UI, not only in the registry.
5. **Free/paid matrix.** A compact feature table separating free from PRO on both the PRO page and the pharma site keeps the value proposition explicit. Relevant if InterMED ever documents its own (non-commercial, no-account) scope against commercial competitors.
6. **Favorites as account-linked sync.** Favorites (tools, drugs, ICD-10 codes) sync across devices through the account (privacy policy). InterMED deliberately keeps favorites local without an account (requirements 90–94); the pattern is worth noting only as a future opt-in-account argument (roadmap phase 4), not a change.
7. **Professional gating.** Prescriber-only sections, professional verification at registration, and specialty-based content personalization. InterMED's clinical-reviewer model and MDR assessment make mandatory professional accounts a heavier decision (phase 4 gate); no adoption recommended now.

## 5. Recommendations (ranked by value/effort)

Each recommendation names its mapping (milestone or backlog) and its gate. Nothing here authorizes implementation, procurement or contact.

1. **Keep and finish the link-first regulatory-reference design (M7 Part B).** Mediately monetizes SmPC/reimbursement content, which confirms clinician demand; InterMED's differentiation is provenance rather than volume. Complete the detail route with links to the official RCP/prospect documents (ANMDMR-linked), explicit "offline unavailable" states for links, and no bundled PDF bodies until rights are established. This is the existing design in requirement 44–45 and the three-option framing in [INTERACTION_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT §"Is SmPC section 4.5 a legally safe fallback?"](../INTERACTION_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT.md). **Gate:** owner sign-off on the bounded scope; ANMDMR linking/retention terms (a rights-holder question drafted, not sent).
2. **Assess CNAS reimbursement/compensation lists as an open-data enrichment dataset (M5/M6 source-registry work).** CNAS is cited by Mediately as a Romanian medicine source, and reimbursement data is one category in their commercial licensing product. Neither fact shows that CNAS publishes the lists openly or in a machine-readable format, so the lists are an **unverified candidate**: locating the official CNAS publications and checking their format is the first step of the assessment. Add a rights assessment (licence, attribution, update cadence, machine format) to the [DATA_SOURCES](../../plan/DATA_SOURCES.md) registry as a separate optional enrichment source — never merged into the Nomenclator snapshot without review. **Gate:** rights verification; any contact with CNAS or a vendor requires explicit owner authorization. Do not assume open access equals redistribution rights.
3. **Build the surgery/ICU calculator candidate shortlist for owner selection (M11 backlog; candidates only).** Mediately's catalog shows the clinical expectation set. Relevant candidates for a surgical/ICU reference, each already requiring a named primary source, units/population limits, version, boundary tests and clinical review per the existing M11 process: Caprini (already requirement 103), SOFA and qSOFA, NEWS2, Glasgow–Blatchford, Alvarado, PSI/PORT, CURB-65, Wells PE, Geneva (revised and prophylaxis), PESI/sPESI, Padua, APACHE II, GCS, BSA chemo-dosing display. **No implementation now.** **Gate:** owner selection from [CALCULATOR_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT](../CALCULATOR_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT.md) plus per-formula review/rights; renal/hepatic restriction tools additionally need the prescribing-restriction gate in section 4 (a licensed source or permitted RCP excerpts, plus an MDR intended-purpose review).
4. **Adopt the calculator transparency pattern in the UI (M11 UI decisions).** Show, per calculator: version, primary reference with access date, units/population limits, the educational warning banner, and print/PDF-friendly results. InterMED already computes classification from the unrounded value with a display-boundary note and per-component approval records (M11 evidence); surfacing them is low effort and high trust value. **Gate:** owner decision on wording/presentation versions (the M11 approval process already separates presentation versions).
5. **Apply the interaction-checker UX contract to M10 acceptance.** Substance-centric multi-select with a documented bound (Mediately uses 2–40), explicit provider attribution, visible "no record within evaluated coverage" semantics, and no universal "safe" verdict. This aligns with requirements 52–56 and 60–64; the competitor evidence is useful acceptance-criteria justification. **Gate:** M10 release remains blocked on the approved provider decision; no changes to clinical logic without the licensed source.
6. **Protect and publicize the offline-first, no-account difference.** Mediately gates full offline access behind PRO/subscription and requires professional registration. InterMED's free, account-free, offline-capable reference is the clearest differentiator for Romanian clinicians; ensure milestone-12 evidence (first-offline honesty, eviction, multitab, rollback) is included in the public documentation. **Gate:** M12 hardening evidence; no scope change.
7. **Record the licensed-vendor route in the source registry; do not contact without authorization.** Mediately publicly sells structured drug data (SPCs, reimbursement information, prescribing restrictions) for Romania, proving that licensed Romanian-content vendors exist. Add this observation to [DATA_SOURCES](../../plan/DATA_SOURCES.md) as a candidate acquisition route (vendor contact, sample, quote, offline/device rights) alongside the interaction-provider shortlist. **Gate:** explicit owner authorization before any outreach; InterMED must not contact vendors without it (the drafted questions in INTERACTION_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT remain unsent).
8. **Defer ICD-10, ATC browse, guidelines and CME to backlog items with rights notes.** Low relevance now; each needs a terminology/copyright assessment before scoping. **Gate:** roadmap decision; no implementation.
9. **Do not adopt the AI-assistant model for clinical content.** Their EULA states the assistant is third-party technology with unreviewed outputs; InterMED's stricter rule (no AI-invented clinical facts; phase-9 gating) is a safety feature to keep, and the contrast is worth documenting in [CLINICAL_SAFETY](../../plan/CLINICAL_SAFETY.md)-adjacent conversations. **Gate:** none; confirm existing policy remains.
10. **Do not adopt mandatory professional registration.** Their account model exists to gate prescriber content and sync favorites; InterMED's no-account MVP is deliberate (requirement 74; roadmap phase 4 opt-in). Record as a considered divergence so future phases do not re-litigate it accidentally. **Gate:** none.

## 6. Risks

- **Reuse prohibition (confirmed on public terms).** The EULA prohibits copying, translation, adaptation, derivative works and third-party distribution without prior written consent, restricts use to personal/household purposes and forbids resale, and Slovenian law applies. This reinforces the standing policy: no Mediately copying or scraping (requirement 22; [DATA_SOURCES](../../plan/DATA_SOURCES.md)). Their data-licensing product is offered for commercial integration; that offer does not extend to InterMED, and InterMED must not use their service as a data proxy.
- **Regulatory posture observed.** No CE marking, EUDAMED registration or medical-device status claim was observed on any reviewed page. The product is positioned as educational/informational with per-tool warnings and a broad EULA disclaimer, while its pharma-facing marketing pages appear to describe clinical-decision-support use (drug interactions, ICD-10, dosing tools); the exact page carrying that wording was not recorded, so this point is **unverified**. That gap between marketing and disclaimer is exactly the intended-purpose analysis InterMED documents in [REGULATORY_ASSESSMENT_MDR](../REGULATORY_ASSESSMENT_MDR.md): a competitor's positioning neither settles InterMED's qualification nor creates precedent; labels do not remove MDR obligations. **Inference** from reviewed pages only; their regulatory file is unknown.
- **Third-party AI dependency.** Their assistant is third-party technology with disclaimed accuracy and monitored interactions. Copying that pattern would conflict with InterMED's clinical-safety rules.
- **Commercial-provider entanglement.** Their interaction content is attributed to a commercial provider (Medbase); using Mediately's checker as an unofficial knowledge source would import both rights and clinical-review problems.
- **Advertising in a clinical tool.** Banner, pop-up and tool-sponsorship formats sit inside clinical workflows, with self-described EFPIA-aware HCP-only targeting. InterMED's non-commercial funding avoids this, but the pattern reinforces why source independence matters for trust and for the MDR content-governance file.
- **Scale asymmetry.** 350,000+ claimed users, 14 markets and a sales-driven data product mean InterMED cannot win on coverage; the strategy that follows from this analysis is provenance, offline-first access, no account, Romanian-specific review and honest coverage limits.
