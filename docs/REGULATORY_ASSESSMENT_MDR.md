## Qualification: intended purpose comes first

MDR **Article 2(1)** includes software intended by its manufacturer for specified medical purposes, including diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease, and investigation of physiological or pathological processes/states. **Article 2(12)** defines intended purpose by the manufacturer's labelling, instructions, promotional/sales materials or statements and clinical evaluation. The assessment must therefore cover the actual intended use and claims across the UI, documentation and public description, rather than one disclaimer. [MDR Article 2(1), (12)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02017R0745-20260719).

For InterMED, phrases such as clinical decision support, checking a patient's regimen, identifying contraindicated combinations, interpreting a patient-specific score or supporting dose selection require medical-purpose assessment. Educational/reference wording can support a non-device conclusion only where the function and intended use actually stay within that boundary. Clinician oversight, manual input, ephemeral inputs, offline execution and an “information only” label do not by themselves establish that a decision-support function is outside MDR. This is an inference from Article 2(1)/(12) and MDCG sections 3.1–3.2; absence of stored patient identifiers is a privacy choice, not a qualification test.

### MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1 decision steps

Apply **Figure 1 and its accompanying steps, section 3.3, pp.12–13** (document page labels) to each function and its dependencies:

1. Is the product software as defined in the guidance? InterMED's PWA is software.
2. Is it an Annex XVI device, an accessory, or software driving/influencing a medical device? If so, follow that regulatory route. No such purpose is established for these functions; verify it before excluding this branch.
3. Does it act on data beyond storage, archival, communication, simple search or lossless compression? Unaltered reference retrieval may stop here; calculations and clinical interpretation require further assessment.
4. Is the action for individual patients' benefit? Generic literature/teaching may stop here; a clinician's selected regimen or patient measurements may satisfy this step without a named patient record.
5. Does its intended purpose meet the MDSW definition? Apply Article 2(1); a calculation is not automatically medical merely because it is software.

Section 3.1 cautions that a search contributing to a medical purpose is not automatically “simple search”. Neither low apparent complexity nor a familiar formula decides qualification. Record the rationale for each answer. [MDCG 2019-11 Rev.1, sections 3.1–3.3](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf).

For later renal/laboratory tools, also apply **Figure 2, section 3.4** to determine MDR versus IVDR: data origin, intended purpose and the significance of in-vitro diagnostic information matter. This document does not settle that boundary for future eGFR/CrCl or laboratory-based scores; Rule 11 applies only if the function falls under MDR. Manual transcription of a laboratory result must not be assumed to settle the question. [MDCG section 3.4](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf).

## Classification: Rule 11 after qualification

Under **Annex VIII, section 6.3, Rule 11**, software providing information used for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions is **class IIa**, except where the decisions' impact may cause:

- **Class III:** death or irreversible deterioration of health.
- **Class IIb:** serious deterioration of health or surgical intervention.

Software intended to monitor physiological processes is class IIa; monitoring vital physiological parameters whose variations could pose immediate danger is class IIb. The remaining Rule 11 category is class I. That residual category does **not** make every non-device reference app a class I device or make every simple medical calculator class I. First qualify the function and consider all applicable classification rules; Annex VIII implementing rules 3.1, 3.3 and 3.5 address intended purpose, software relationships and the strictest applicable rule/sub-rule. [MDR Annex VIII](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02017R0745-20260719), [MDCG sections 4.1–4.2.1](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf).

Class IIa is a **starting hypothesis**, not a final class for all InterMED clinical functions. Evaluate how an incorrect or missing result affects the intended decision, clinical situation and population. A higher class is not determined solely by the seriousness of a disease or by the owner being a surgeon. Conversely, a clinician confirming the output does not automatically remove Rule 11. [MDCG section 4.2.1 and Annex III](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/b45335c5-1679-4c71-a91c-fc7a4d37f12b_en?filename=mdcg_2019_11_en.pdf).

## Non-commercial status does not exempt InterMED from MDR

**Non-commercial, free-of-charge and open-source status do NOT provide a general MDR exemption.** Article 2(1)/(12) tests intended medical purpose, not revenue. Article 2(27) expressly includes free supply in its definition of making available **in the course of a commercial activity**; preserve that qualification rather than equating every free publication with commercial activity. Article 2(28) defines first making available as placing on the market, and Article 2(29) addresses making a device ready for its intended use by the final user for the first time. Article 5(1) requires compliance before placing on the market **or putting into service**; Article 5(4) also treats manufacture/use within health institutions as putting into service. The concrete manufacturer, distribution and service arrangements need advice; absence of payment cannot settle them. [MDR Articles 2 and 5](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02017R0745-20260719).

**Article 5(5) is a narrow health-institution route, not an exemption for a surgeon's public app.** It concerns devices manufactured and used only within Union health institutions, with all conditions met: no transfer to another legal entity; appropriate quality management; documented unmet needs compared with equivalent marketed devices; competent-authority information; public declaration; design/manufacturing/performance documentation; manufacture consistent with that documentation; and review of clinical experience/corrective action. Relevant Annex I safety/performance requirements still apply. Industrial-scale manufacture is excluded, and Member States may impose further information requirements or restrictions. [MDR Article 5(5)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02017R0745-20260719).

No evidence establishes that InterMED or its owner meets this institutional route. General distribution to independent clinicians/devices must not be assumed to meet the same-legal-entity restriction. A surgeon's clinical review or hospital affiliation alone is insufficient. [MDCG 2023-1 sections 3.2, 3.4–3.6](https://health.ec.europa.eu/document/download/05b15d55-1bcf-4e17-99c4-15c706325847_en?filename=mdcg_2023-1_en.pdf).

## What must not be released before a conformity decision

Do not release the multi-drug checker or patient-specific calculators for diagnostic/therapeutic use, or reference functions presented/integrated as clinical decision support, before a documented qualification/classification and conformity-route decision and completion of the applicable conformity obligations. A decision to pursue conformity is not completed conformity. A separately bounded non-device reference release still needs documented rationale, rights clearance, explicit owner clinical approval per component/version and the project's remaining release gates. “Preliminary”, “MVP”, “information only” and non-commercial labels cannot waive these gates. [MDR Articles 5(1), 52](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:02017R0745-20260719), [CLINICAL_SAFETY](../plan/CLINICAL_SAFETY.md).
